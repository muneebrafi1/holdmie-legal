Privacy Policy

Last updated 13 November 2026 · Effective 13 November 2026

Holdmie is an app for two people on iPhone. Almost everything you put into it is meant for your partner, so this policy says plainly what you share, what reaches your partner and when, what reaches our servers and the companies that help us run them, how long it is kept, and the choices you have.

The short version: what you make in Holdmie is stored on our servers so it can reach your partner’s phone and widgets, and only the two of you can see it. Location and status stay off until you both turn them on, and one tap stops them. Holdmie has no ads and no tracking, analytics or advertising tools, and we don’t sell your information. You can delete your account in the app at any time.

Who we are

Holdmie is made by Muneeb Rafi, an individual developer in Pakistan (“we”, “us”). We decide how the information described here is used, which makes us its “controller” under data protection laws such as the GDPR. Contact: muneebrafishakoor@gmail.com.

What you put into the app

Your account. Holdmie starts with a guest account so you can begin right away. To keep using it, you sign in with Apple or with Google. Sign in with Apple gives us your name, which you can edit, and an email address, which can be Apple’s private relay address if you choose Hide My Email. Google gives us your name, email address and profile picture. We never see your Apple or Google password.

Your profile. The display name you choose and, if you add one, a profile photo.

What you make and send. Drawings (as pictures and as the strokes behind them), photos and short videos with their captions (a video keeps its sound if you allowed the microphone), the marks and emoji you leave on your partner’s photos, answers to questions, messages, to-dos, bucket list items and their photos, countdowns, your anniversary date, hugs and other affection taps, “thinking of you” taps, game moves and quick reactions in games (Love, Haha, Ouch, Nice one), and the drawings and guesses in Draw & Guess. Drawings you save to your Gallery without sending are stored too, and only you can see them. Before a photo leaves your phone, Holdmie makes a smaller copy without the hidden details cameras add, including where the photo was taken. A short video is copied the same way, without where it was filmed.

Your choices. Your app language, notification switches, the “Show on my Home Screen” switch, kilometres or miles, the location switches described below, and what you picked when Holdmie asked what you want from it (for example “A little spice”, which decides whether flirty questions can appear in your daily set).

Reports and messages to us. When you report something or write to us, we receive what you write and anything attached to it. Report a bug in More, and “Widget not updating?” on the Widgets page, open your mail app with details filled in for us: your app version, iOS version, iPhone model and User ID, and for widgets also which widgets you’ve placed and when they last refreshed. You see everything before you send it, and nothing goes until you send the email yourself.

Your partner’s nickname and your relationship stage, which setup asks for, stay on your phone and are never sent to us.

What your partner sees, and when

Pairing joins your account to one other person’s, and it takes both of you: one of you shares a code, the other types it, and the person who shared the code confirms with one tap or taps “Not me”. To decide, they see the name and photo of the person who typed it. The person who typed it sees nothing about the code’s owner until the pairing is confirmed.

Once you’re paired, your partner sees your display name and profile photo and everything you send or share in Holdmie: drawings, photos, videos and captions, marks and emoji on photos, messages, affection and “thinking of you” taps, the shared to-do list, bucket list, countdowns and anniversary, and the games you play together. When you pair, the lists, countdowns and anniversary you set up on your own move into your shared space, where your partner can see them. Your partner can also save what you send to their own photo library or take a screenshot. Those copies are outside Holdmie, so they stay with your partner even after you delete something, unpair or delete your account.

Answers to questions stay hidden from your partner until you’ve both answered. Then you both see both answers and can talk about them.

Your partner can see when something they sent has reached your phone (“Delivered”). Holdmie has no read receipts.

Because your streak counts the days you both did something, your partner can see whether you’ve done something in Holdmie today.

While you have Holdmie open, your partner’s app can show that you’re there, for example as a small dot on the map or in a game. It clears a few minutes after you leave the app. Holdmie never shows whether your screen is on or which other apps you use.

Nobody else sees your shared space. There are no public profiles, and other Holdmie users can’t find you.

Notifications and widgets show your partner’s name and pictures on your Lock Screen and Home Screen, and the notification for a message or a photo shows what your partner wrote, a photo’s caption included, where anyone holding your phone may see them. Turn off “Show words in notifications” in More › Notifications and those notifications say only “Sent you a message” or “Sent you a photo”; iOS’s own Show Previews setting can also keep notification text off your Lock Screen. Turn off “Show on my Home Screen” and your widgets and notifications stop showing your partner’s pictures. You choose which notifications you get in More › Notifications.

When Lock Screen drawings are available in Holdmie, they’re off until you turn them on (Widgets › Lock Screen, or More › Notifications). While they’re on, each drawing your partner sends shows as a card (an iOS Live Activity) on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island for 30 minutes, where anyone who sees your phone can see it. Your partner can see whether you’ve turned Lock Screen drawings on and your phone can show the card, and when a drawing they sent is showing on your Lock Screen. When your partner draws for your Lock Screen, Holdmie shows them the picture you picked for it in the app (a photo you two share, or plain black), never your own wallpaper. Choose “Only show ‘[name] drew this for you’” and the card shows your partner’s name and no drawing. You can hide a card at any time, turn Lock Screen drawings off in Holdmie, or turn off Holdmie’s Live Activities in iPhone Settings.

If you’ve turned on Sensitive Content Warning or Communication Safety in iOS, Holdmie checks the photos and drawings your partner sends on your iPhone and blurs any that may be sensitive until you tap. While that setting is on, your notifications and widgets show words instead of your partner’s pictures. The check runs on your iPhone, and the picture isn’t sent anywhere for it.

Location and status sharing

Location and status sharing is off until you turn it on, and Holdmie never asks for it during setup. Nothing about your location or your phone’s status is stored or shown until both of you have switched sharing on. Neither of you can turn it on for the other, and your partner can ask you to share at most once a day.

What can be shared, each with its own switch: your position on a map you share and the distance between you; your battery level, rounded to the nearest 5% and shown as “about 60%”, and whether you’re charging; your network type (Wi-Fi, mobile data or offline); the name of the Wi-Fi network you’re on; and a 7-day history. When you switch sharing on, position, battery and network type are on and you can turn any of them off. Wi-Fi name and history are off by default and stay off unless you turn them on; Wi-Fi name also needs precise location. Each item is shown only when you have both switched it on, and you both see the same things about each other. Your partner’s app also learns which of these switches you have on and whether you’ve allowed “Update when closed”.

Your partner sees how old your latest reading is, worded like “Updated 7m ago”. Holdmie never claims you’re awake, busy or free.

How readings are taken. Holdmie sends a reading when you open it, and every 30 seconds while you have the map open; your partner having their map open doesn’t make yours update. If you also choose “Update when closed” (iOS’s Always permission, an optional extra step), iOS wakes Holdmie briefly after you’ve moved a significant distance or arrived at or left a place, and the app sends one small reading. When your partner opens the map, or switches sharing on while yours is on, iOS may also wake Holdmie briefly while it’s closed, at most twice an hour, to send your battery and network, your Wi-Fi name if you share it, and the last position iOS has for your phone if it’s less than an hour old. This can happen with While Using permission too. Holdmie doesn’t follow you continuously and doesn’t use iOS’s continuous background location mode. Battery and network readings go with each of these readings.

What we store. Only your latest reading, replaced each time: usually a new position at most every 5 minutes, or every 30 seconds while you have the map open. If you’ve both turned history on, we also keep a point whenever you’re at least 10 minutes and 100 metres from the last one, rounded to about 100 metres, for 7 days. Place names in the history are looked up at city level on the phone showing them, through Apple’s map service, and we don’t store them. Widgets get the distance between you, never your position. A position nobody has refreshed for 30 days is deleted.

Stopping. One tap stops sharing, on the map or in Location settings, with no confirmation and no reason asked. When you stop, your position, history and status are deleted from our servers right away. Your partner isn’t notified when you stop; they see “Not sharing right now”. When someone turns sharing on, the other person is told. Turning history off deletes your kept places, and “Clear” deletes the history for both of you. While you share, Holdmie reminds you every 30 days that sharing is on, and that reminder can’t be turned off. Unpairing and deleting your account stop sharing and delete it all, and sharing pauses while neither of you has an active plan.

iOS lists Holdmie under Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services, and Safety Check can remove its access at any time. Then your partner sees “Location isn’t updating”, with no reason given. The map itself is drawn by Apple Maps.

Permissions the app asks for

Camera: to take a photo or a short video to send to your partner. Microphone: only while you hold the shutter to film a video, for its sound; Holdmie asks once, before your first video, and if you say no your videos are silent. Photos: to choose a photo to send; through iOS’s photo picker, Holdmie gets only the photos you pick. Adding to Photos: only when you choose to save a picture, and the app can add pictures but not read your library. Location: While Using for the shared map, and Always only if you choose “Update when closed”. Notifications: so you know when your partner sends something. Motion: iOS asks apps that include its location tools to explain motion access, but Holdmie never reads your motion. Wi-Fi network name: read only while you share it.

Saying no to a permission turns off only the feature that needs it. Holdmie doesn’t use your contacts, calendar or health data. You can change any permission in iPhone Settings › Holdmie.

Information your phone sends automatically

To run Holdmie, the app, its widgets and its notification handler send us: your account ID (a random identifier, shown in the app as your User ID or Support ID, which you can give us for support); an identifier the app makes for each install; push notification tokens for your phone and its widgets; on iOS 17.2 and later, once Lock Screen drawings are available in Holdmie, the token iOS gives Holdmie to start its Lock Screen card (a Live Activity), sent with your iOS version and whether its More Frequent Updates setting is on, even while you have Lock Screen drawings off; the token of each Lock Screen card that shows on your phone; which Holdmie widgets you’ve placed, so we send widget updates only to phones that use them; the app version; your app language; your time zone, so days, streaks and reminders follow your own clock (your partner sees your local time only if you choose to show it); and whether the app is open right now.

Like any online service, our servers see your phone’s IP address with each request. We use it to keep Holdmie safe, for example to limit guesses at pairing codes. Our own counters forget it within 3 days, and our hosting provider keeps it in its security and request logs for a limited time.

If Holdmie closes because of an error, the details are saved on your phone only, so the app can show you what happened the next time it opens. They reach us only if you copy them into a message yourself.

What stays on your phone

Drafts (drawings, answers and messages not sent yet), a copy of your space so the app opens quickly and works offline, your settings, your partner’s nickname and relationship stage, the answer from Apple’s age check, crash details, and what the widgets show, kept in storage shared only with Holdmie’s own widgets and notification handler. If you turn on the daily reminder, it’s scheduled on your phone for the time you pick.

A separate key lets the widgets and notifications reach our servers. It can only read your shared space and send a few small actions (a to-do tick, a hug, a status reading), and it stops working when you sign out or delete your account. Deleting the app removes what it stored on your phone except that key, which iOS keeps in its keychain until you install Holdmie again, when it’s discarded. Left there, it stops working by itself within 90 days, or at once if you delete your account.

Purchases

Holdmie Pro and gem packs are bought through Apple’s App Store. Apple handles your payment; we never see your card or bank details. We use RevenueCat to confirm purchases and to keep your plan in step across your phones and with your partner. RevenueCat receives your Holdmie account ID, your purchase and subscription records from Apple, and basic information about your phone and the app, such as the iOS version, app version, store country and IP address.

We store your plan’s state (which plan, when it renews or ends, whether it’s in a free trial, billing problems) and your gem balance with a record of every change. Your partner sees that your plan covers them and your name (“Included with [your name]’s plan”), never prices or payment details.

Who handles your information for us

We use a small number of service providers. Supabase, RevenueCat and Expo handle your information only on our instructions and only for the jobs described here, under data processing terms that require them to protect it at least as well as this policy does. Apple (sign-in, notifications, maps and the App Store) and Google (sign-in and our support email) act as independent companies under their own privacy policies.

Supabase, Inc. stores our database, files and sign-in service in Amazon Web Services data centres in the eastern United States (region us-east-1), and everything you make in Holdmie is stored there. Requests reach it through Supabase’s network, and some of its server functions, and the live connection that carries games, live touches and whether you’re in the app, can run in a Supabase data centre nearer to you, which passes your information through without storing it.

RevenueCat, Inc. (United States) checks and records purchases, as described above.

Apple delivers notifications and widget updates (Apple Push Notification service), runs Sign in with Apple and the App Store, draws the map, looks up place names, and answers the age check where the law requires it. If you turn on Lock Screen drawings, the card goes to your phone through Apple’s push service, which carries your partner’s name and, unless you chose “Only show ‘[name] drew this for you’”, the lines of the drawing. If we later let the card fill in while your partner draws, the drawing so far travels the same way.

Expo, a service of 650 Industries, Inc. (United States), passes our notifications on to Apple. It receives your phone’s push token and the notification: your partner’s name, a short line such as “Sent you a doodle” or “Sent you a message” (never the text of a message or a caption), and the identifiers your phone needs to open the right screen. An emoji your partner leaves on your photo is part of its notification’s line while your “Show words in notifications” is on, so it travels through Expo and Apple like any notification text; with it off, the line only says your partner reacted. Pictures in notifications, and the words of a message or a photo’s caption while “Show words in notifications” is on, are fetched by your phone directly from our servers when the notification arrives, so they never pass through Apple’s or Expo’s push servers.

Google LLC runs Sign in with Google, if you choose it, and hosts our support email, so what you send us and our replies pass through Gmail.

We may also disclose information when the law requires it, when it’s needed to protect someone from serious harm, or as part of a sale or transfer of Holdmie, in which case this policy keeps applying to your information.

What we don’t do

Holdmie has no ads and no advertising, analytics, attribution or crash-reporting tools. We don’t track you across other companies’ apps or websites, so Holdmie never shows Apple’s tracking prompt. We don’t sell your personal information or share it for advertising, and we don’t build profiles of you. We don’t use what you make to train AI models.

We don’t read your conversations or look at your drawings and photos. Someone on our side sees content only when it’s part of a report, when you send it to us, or when the law requires it.

Why we use it

To provide Holdmie as you asked (our contract with you): your account, pairing, sending things to your partner, keeping both phones in step, notifications, widgets, games, your plan and gems.

With your consent: location and status sharing and each of its switches, Lock Screen drawings, and access to your camera, microphone, photos and notifications. You can withdraw consent at any time by turning the switch off or in iPhone Settings; that doesn’t affect what happened before.

Some of what you share, such as answers to flirty questions, intimate photos, or simply who your partner is, can reveal your sex life or sexual orientation, which data protection laws treat as especially sensitive. We use it only to deliver it to your partner and keep it for the two of you, never to profile you. We rely on your consent, which you give by choosing to share it with your partner and, for flirty questions, by choosing “A little spice”. You can withdraw it at any time by deleting what you shared, unpairing or deleting your account; that doesn’t affect what happened before.

For our legitimate interests in keeping Holdmie safe and working: limits against abuse, handling reports, fixing errors, and removing guest accounts that were left behind.

To meet legal duties, such as answering lawful requests and keeping records the law requires.

Signing in with Apple or Google is needed to keep using Holdmie, and pairing is needed to share with your partner; without them we can’t provide it. Everything else you put in is your choice. We don’t make decisions about you based only on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects. Automatic limits, such as a wait after too many wrong pairing codes, only protect the service.

How long we keep it

Your account and your shared space: while you use Holdmie, until you delete your account.

Drawings: after 180 days we delete the full-size picture and its strokes, and keep only the smaller picture used for widgets.

Games: drawings from Draw & Guess for 24 hours; moves, including Draw & Guess guesses, for 7 days after the game ends; nudges to play for 30 days. Reactions are stored with the game: only the latest one each of you sent, cleared when a new game starts. Results of finished games stay with your space.

Location: your latest reading until it’s replaced, until you stop sharing, or 30 days without a new one; history for 7 days.

Things you delete disappear from both phones, and their photos and drawings are deleted from storage within minutes. A short record that the item was deleted, which keeps phones in step, stays for up to 90 days.

After unpairing: hidden from both of you for 30 days, then deleted (see below). Guest accounts that never signed in and aren’t paired: deleted after 30 days without use.

Technical records: abuse counters 3 days, delivery records (“Delivered”) 60 days, records of widget and notification actions (so a tap counts once) 30 days, pairing requests 30 days, server error records 90 days, purchase notices from RevenueCat 180 days.

Reports: as long as we need them to handle the report and to keep people safe, then deleted.

Backups: our hosting provider keeps daily backups of the database for 7 days, so deleted information can remain in a backup until it rolls over.

Apple, Google, RevenueCat and Expo keep what they receive under their own policies.

Unpairing, blocking and deleting your account

Unpairing. Either of you can unpair, alone and at once. Location, status and history are deleted for both of you right away, games in progress end, and Lock Screen drawings turn off for both of you; the tokens that start the card are deleted, and each card’s own token once that card has closed. You each keep your own copy of the to-do list, the bucket list with its photos, and the countdowns, including items the other person added. Drawings (including the ones in your Gallery, sent or not), photos, answers and messages are hidden from both of you for 30 days in case you pair with each other again, which brings everything back. After 30 days they’re deleted, and either of you can delete them sooner. Your partner gets a short, neutral note and notification. If one of you paid, the plan stays with the payer and the other person’s access ends.

Blocking. When you unpair, report someone or tap “Not me” on a pairing request, you can also block that person. Their pairing code is refused from then on, and they aren’t told why. You can unblock them in More › Blocked.

Deleting your account. You can delete your account in the app (More › Delete account, and Account on the plan screen). It happens right away, with no email or waiting period. We delete your account, sign-in, profile and profile photo, location and status, gem balance, plan records, device tokens and pairing codes, delete your customer record at RevenueCat and, if you used Sign in with Apple, cancel Holdmie’s access to your Apple Account. If you used Google, you can also remove Holdmie in your Google Account settings. We keep only what safety or the law needs: reports, as described under How long we keep it, purchase notices from RevenueCat for up to 180 days, and database backups until they roll over within 7 days. Technical records listed there run out on their own schedule.

What your partner keeps. Your drawings, photos, answers and messages become “deleted” placeholders in their space, and their files are deleted. To-dos, bucket list items and countdowns you added, including photos you put on bucket list items, stay in their lists without your name, because they belong to the shared space. The hugs and taps you sent stay in their counts, also without your name. A former partner keeps their own copy of the lists from when you were paired, as described under Unpairing. Your partner gets a short, neutral note. If you paid for the plan, it stops covering them.

Deleting your account doesn’t cancel an App Store subscription. Apple keeps billing until you cancel, so the app shows a Manage subscription button before you delete.

After an unpair, a block or a deletion, we ask the other phone to clear Holdmie’s widgets and old notifications and to end any Lock Screen card. If that phone is offline or has notifications off, the last picture can stay on its widget until the widget next refreshes or Holdmie opens there.

Reports and safety

You can report any message, answer, photo or drawing by pressing and holding it and choosing Report, and report your partner from More. A report sends us a copy of the item, the reason you chose, anything you write and both account IDs, so we can act even if the item is deleted later. A person reads every report and replies by email, to the address on your account if it has one. To tell us something isn’t working, use Report a bug in More, which opens your mail app as described above.

We may remove content and limit or close accounts that break our terms. Where the law requires it, or someone may be at risk of serious harm, we may pass information to the authorities. Content that sexually exploits children is reported to the authorities.

Your choices and rights

In the app you can edit your name and photo, delete what you sent, clear location history, stop sharing, choose notifications, turn off pictures on your Home Screen, unpair, block and delete your account. In iPhone Settings you can turn off Holdmie’s access to location, camera, microphone, photos and notifications, and its Live Activities, at any time.

Depending on where you live (for example the European Union, the United Kingdom, Brazil, Japan, South Korea, or California and other US states), you may have the right to know what we hold about you and get a copy, to correct it, to delete it, to take it with you, to object to or restrict some uses, and to withdraw consent. Email muneebrafishakoor@gmail.com from the address on your account, or include your User ID, and we’ll answer within one month, or sooner where your local law sets a shorter time, such as 10 days in South Korea and 15 days in Brazil. We may need to confirm it’s you. Some information belongs to both of you, such as a conversation, so we may need to take your partner’s rights into account. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

We don’t sell or share personal information as US state privacy laws define those words, and we use precise location only to provide the sharing you turned on. We won’t treat you differently for using your rights.

Where your information is processed

We’re based in Pakistan. Our database and files are in the United States, and our providers may process information in other countries, including Supabase data centres near you for some server functions and live connections. Where the law requires safeguards for these transfers, we rely on the protections our providers offer in their data processing terms, such as the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses.

Security

Information travels between Holdmie and our servers encrypted, and our hosting provider stores it encrypted. Database rules let only you and your partner read your shared space and only you read your account details, and location readings are kept where no phone can read them directly. Pairing codes and the widgets’ key are stored only in scrambled (hashed) form. There are no Holdmie passwords to steal: you sign in with Apple or Google.

Holdmie isn’t end-to-end encrypted: our servers hold what you share so that your partner’s phone and widgets can fetch it, even while your phone is off. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach puts you at risk, we’ll tell you as the law requires.

Children

Holdmie is for people 16 and older and isn’t directed at children. We don’t knowingly collect information from anyone under 16. Where the law requires it, for example in some US states, Holdmie asks Apple for your age range when you first open it on iOS 26.2 or later. Apple tells the app only whether you are 16 or older, never your birth date, and the answer stays on your phone. If you’re under 16, the app stops there. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 16, we delete it. If you think a child is using Holdmie, email muneebrafishakoor@gmail.com.
